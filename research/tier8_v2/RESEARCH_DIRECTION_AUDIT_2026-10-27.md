# Direction and hypothesis audit — 27 October 2026

Assessment requested by the user. This is a review of completed evidence, not a
new preregistration, statistical test, adopted method, tier freeze or instruction
to interrupt the current evaluations.

## 1. Alignment: useful development, but material scope drift

README/METHODOLOGY define the headline as smaller **full-precision resident MAS**
versus larger **quantized SAS** at equal effective RSS/token ceilings, with **16 GB
as the primary tier** and 8/32 GB as supporting tiers. They also specify financial
grounding/invention/mismatch outcomes and KYC/credit tasks.

The redevelopment legitimately fixes baseline weaknesses and tests review/repair.
But the current financial QA study compares **quantized 3B MAS with quantized 7B
SAS**, uses numerical answer/delivery metrics, and has become an extended 8 GB
adaptation/verification/preprocessing investigation. Its results do not directly
test the original precision contrast, primary tier or all finance-workflow claims.
Rule-solvable synthetic packs cannot substitute for a credible final task.

The additional known-method prototypes and repeated integrity checks have been
useful, but letting novelty remain an indefinitely expanding prerequisite for
higher tiers is no longer a good allocation of effort. The agent allowed that
development loop to become too open-ended. More code, lower training loss or a
successful memory fit does not establish the requested scientific contribution.

## 2. Evidence: promising local SAS result, no broad hypothesis established

Completed automated analyses replayed full prompts, source execution, scores,
token ledgers, memory and application proofs. This direction review reconciled
their counts and current source-code identities; it made no new neural calls.

| Completed direct SAS condition, 52 selection cases | Reported correct/wrong/withheld | Raw correct/wrong/withheld |
|---|---|---|
| Original joint adapter |8 /25 /19|10 /23 /19|
| Matched continuation control |8 /23 /21|10 /21 /21|
| Crossed continuation |10 /16 /26|12 /14 /26|

Against the original, crossed has two more correct and nine fewer wrong deliveries
under both metrics. The report-bootstrap interval for wrong-delivery difference
is approximately −28.9 to −5.6 percentage points; for correct deliveries it is
0 to +9.7 points. This supports a narrow exploratory error-reduction finding,
not an established positive correct-answer effect. The comparison includes extra
training; the matched control is needed to isolate the negative-data intervention.
Versus that matched control, the full-policy error interval touches zero.

On the separate 49-case / 15-report diagnostic partition, SAS control is reported
8/20/21 and crossed 9/20/20; raw is14/14/21 versus15/14/20. The major selection
error reduction does not replicate there. Intervals remain broad; the reserved
comparison does not include the original joint adapter.

MAS on those49 cases changes reported4/17/28 to4/9/36, while raw changes8/13/28
to7/6/36. Fewer wrong deliveries come with more withholding and one fewer raw-
correct delivery. It fails the existing adoption requirement under both metrics.
The earlier selection comparison also lost one correct answer against the strong
original critic. A generally beneficial intervention for both architectures is
therefore not established.

Control-SAS repair demonstrably added two wrong deliveries with no correct-
delivery gain and19.1 minutes recorded stage work. Two initially numerically
correct drafts were rejected, then wrong revisions accepted, within one report.
This is a useful conditional failure finding, not proof repair always fails or
a new discovery of the general self-correction problem.

These are one-seed, public training-source report holdouts and multiple exploratory
contrasts. They are not untouched publication confirmation. Failure to establish
superiority does not establish equality or accept the null. A claim of practical
equivalence needs a justified, prospectively specified negligible-effect margin
and adequate precision; do not select that margin to fit current results.
[Primary statistical reference](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/).

## 3. Recommendation: close 8 GB development, then prioritize 16 GB

1. Finish the **two remaining already-planned SAS prompt evaluations** and package
   the completed comparisons. Keep all negative results and the current criteria.
2. Stop adding 8 GB features/training sweeps or launching the prepared header/
   catalog studies merely to pursue a positive novelty result. Leave those
   unvalidated prototypes outside the frozen main method or as future work.
3. Before higher-tier outcomes, reconcile the exact paper question, tasks,
   precision contrast, resident model allocations, adaptation policy, prompts,
   tools, repair variants, primary endpoints and resource measurements in one
   prospective tier matrix. Explicitly label any amendment to the original design.
   Neither an FP16 MAS label for Q4 models nor numerical accuracy labelled as
   invention/mismatch recall is acceptable. Do not silently reuse old tier YAMLs.
4. Freeze the methods and run **16 GB as the primary experiment**, using a credible
   held-out task and appropriate rule/tool baselines. Keep developed/tuned reports
   separate from confirmation; no post-hoc choice of metric or favorable cases.
   Retain fixed conditions/ablations that distinguish generation, verification
   and repair. Do not claim unlimited optimization or isolated architecture effects.
5. Run **32 GB as the planned scaling check**, with suitable hardware. The current
   host exposes about15.44 GB physical RAM: it cannot validate a resident25.6 GB
   RSS tier. Swap or merely raising a configuration cap would not provide that.

The strongest prospective paper direction is a bounded empirical question about
resource allocation and its failure modes. A new algorithm is not the only possible
contribution. Originality and publication strength still require comparison to
closest work, credible tasks, strong baselines and reproducible cross-tier results.
Do not force a method-win narrative or declare the novelty objective achieved.

## Newly added statistical PDF: historical, exploratory interpretation

The remote commitfb98604 adds `TIER_8_V2_STATISTICAL_EVALUATION_REPORT.pdf`;
it was merged without changing the live inference sources. Its snapshot cites
head2e154cb and explicitly says crossed SAS is still running. Therefore it omits
the later completed SAS/original and reserved comparisons above. Its broad
executive statement that no candidate passed the sample Pareto/no-loss rules
needs updating in light of those completed SAS comparisons; this does not imply
overall method adoption or novelty is proven.

Treat its many significance calculations as exploratory. The same questions are
paired and clustered within reports: unpaired tests or bounds over hypothetical
case overlap are not substitutes for the actual paired records, and ordinary
case-wise McNemar/binomial tests do not themselves account for report clustering.
Numerous post-hoc tests without a predeclared testing family need explicit
multiplicity caution. Do not select whichever Wald/bootstrap/exact test gives
the most favorable verdict. We have not recomputed every PDF p-value here.

The PDF's post-hoc association cannot establish a learned semantic mechanism;
a probe at50% with p=1 cannot prove absence of all shortcuts. Exact quartet label
balance is a construction property, which should be distinguished from empirical
proof that all models/feature classes lack shortcuts. Shared-task loss changes
are consistent with transfer, not proof of improved generated QA. Preserve this
report as a historical source and use the updated raw-result audit for decisions.
