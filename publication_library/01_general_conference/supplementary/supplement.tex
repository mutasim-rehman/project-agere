\section{Scope and Evidence Status}
This supplement accompanies the main paper and retains the broader research sequence, including weak baselines, unsuccessful interventions, diagnostic successes, failures to transfer, and resource interruptions. It is organized by evidential role rather than treating every development experiment as a confirmatory trial. Repeated questions, repeated controls and post-hoc diagnostic outputs are not pooled into a sample-size claim. No neural inference was run to prepare either manuscript. Historical files remain unchanged.

The paper package includes an artifact inventory with hashes, an unfiltered collection of 222 result/score/analysis documents selected by filename from the research and preserved-evidence trees, independently regenerated headline metrics, and raw-score checks. The collection is a machine-readable census, not 222 independent experiments. The complete research notes and \path{DECISIONS.md} remain authoritative for chronology. Superseded experiments, failed training fits and incomplete jobs remain in the repository and archives. The historical manuscript is preserved separately and should not be used for the new Ubuntu resource measurements.

\paragraph{Verification levels.}
All 20 handoff archives and their 2,930 mapped source files pass recorded byte-length and SHA-256 checks. The broader inventory records 3,776 research and preserved files, including duplicated evidence representations. Main Ubuntu results were recomputed from raw case ledgers: 1,920 initial/final programs were re-executed and rescored, all 960 records reconciled with the published export, and all stage-generated-token sums checked against the 2,048 ceiling. All 668,817 memory samples from 16 worker attempts pass the recorded RSS and process-swap checks. Fourteen Windows FinQA paired ledgers were re-executed and rescored using the pinned official evaluator and reported-answer scorer; seven TAT-QA prediction ledgers were rescored. The 96-context planner and 28-context secondary MAS outcomes were independently rescored using the official TAT-QA implementation. Older result documents are retained with their original audit provenance; the present checks do not claim independent expert adjudication of every financial label or a fresh replay of every historical training trajectory.

\section{Metrics and Statistical Details}
\paragraph{Historical reported-answer score.}
The evaluator parses supported simple numerical reported labels and their rounding precision. Predicted percentage points are divided by 100; percent labels require percent/fraction output units, while ordinary numerical labels require number/fraction units. The normalized value must fall within the larger of reported rounding tolerance and $10^{-5}$, with a valid ANSWER decision and no execution issues. Unsupported labels remain in the denominator. This historical rule can disagree with the raw execution reference and should not be interpreted as official program equivalence.

\paragraph{Raw execution score.}
For finite predicted $\hat y$ and raw reference $y$, the numerical condition is $|\hat y-y|\leq\max(10^{-5},10^{-5}|y|)$. Correct delivery additionally requires the pipeline to deliver. The stored raw-correct field is a numerical score; source support and review are separate. Primary SAS has 34 deliveries correct by both scores, 44 reported-only, four raw-only and 44 wrong by both. Thus 48/126 delivered answers change correctness label; the two scores are not nested. Primary MAS has five correct by both and three wrong by both.

\paragraph{Official benchmark evaluators.}
Windows FinQA program outputs are translated to the benchmark's arithmetic token representation and evaluated using the pinned implementation, then compared with stored execution labels. Its evaluator hash is \texttt{\seqsplit{845cd131cab843eceff256cf6d392978cc470a7da4a80107beb56027fdca5c13}}. TAT-QA uses the authors' \path{TaTQAEmAndF1} implementation, hash \texttt{\seqsplit{2aeeac479f89f8c76300af1cc0e8d098eb86af84bc386b38b6ab4af484a6dea8}}, with predicted numerical scale. On the reported arithmetic single-number comparisons, summed F1 equals summed exact match; it is not an additional independent success endpoint.

\paragraph{Cluster bootstrap.}
For Study A, let cluster $g$ contain $n_g$ questions and paired difference sum $s_g$. Each draw samples the 50 reports with replacement and computes $\sum_g s_g/\sum_g n_g$ over sampled clusters. We use 10,000 draws, NumPy \path{default_rng(42)}, and linear 2.5/97.5 percentiles. The primary reported correct-delivery interval is $[-0.538961,-0.365517]$ and company-cluster sensitivity is $[-0.534722,-0.364405]$. The corresponding raw interval is $[-0.282609,-0.133720]$. These are resampling estimates under the observed cluster structure, not randomized-treatment causal intervals. The original sealed analysis uses Python RNG/quantile indexing; its original interval $[-0.540373,-0.366906]$ is retained, not overwritten.

Planner paired intervals reproduce the archived Python \path{random.Random(73)} procedure: 10,000 draws of 96 table-context differences, sorted indices 250 and 9749. For gains $b$, losses $c$, the two-sided exact McNemar value is $\min[1,2\sum_{k=0}^{\min(b,c)}\binom{b+c}{k}/2^{b+c}]$. With four gains and zero losses it is 0.125. Empirical resampling cannot invent a loss absent from four observed discordances, explaining why the positive percentile interval must not be presented as strong confirmatory evidence. The zero-width secondary correct-delivery interval is a degenerate empirical bootstrap, not proof of population equality.

Older Windows paired summaries generally use 2,000 company-cluster draws with Python seed 42 and indices 50/1949; we retain that procedure instead of silently substituting a different interval. Exact question-pair tests assume independence of pairs; shared companies may violate it. Older 16 GB supplemental analyses include company sensitivity, equal-report weighting, leave-one-report-out checks and cluster sign-flip tests. These post-hoc analyses are restored in Sections~\ref{sec:extended-a}--\ref{sec:statistics-restored}; sign-flip assumptions are explicit. The new paper does not elevate them into prespecified primary discoveries. No observed-power, noninferiority or equivalence claim is made. No seed/model uncertainty is inferred from a question bootstrap.

\section{Exploratory Sequence: Positive, Null and Adverse Findings}
\subsection{Earlier Linux Development and Training}
The original structured synthetic development suite is solved by a generator-aware deterministic parser (150/150); the 24 temporal KYC probes are also solved by a compact rule control. These are regression/mechanism tests and cannot establish the necessity or superiority of an LLM. A four-case shared-weight pilot improves delivered coverage from zero to one while candidate correctness remains one; patch repair has no gain. Shared residency and successful fitting establish resource behavior, not semantic capability.

On the repeatedly reused first 24 FinQA development questions, the stronger 7B SAS with 128-token analysis and two demonstrations delivers 13 correct, five wrong and withholds six by reported scoring. Longer 256-token analysis yields 11 correct and nine wrong; more table coverage leaves 13 correct. Symbolic cancellation feedback corrects no answers beyond plain retry in a two-trigger pilot. The 3B MAS with analysis/demonstrations produces four reported-correct candidates but delivers three correct, one wrong and withholds 20. Longer analysis does not improve its four correct candidates and increases wrong delivery to ten. These are development comparisons, not optimized-system bounds.

Matched no-analysis/no-demonstration actor adaptation changes 0/24 reported-correct candidates (all refusals) to 11/24, with ten correct, 11 wrong and three withheld. The gain is real on reused data, but starts from a weak floor baseline. A larger unadapted 3B verifier with the same 11 correct drafts withholds all 24, demonstrating that stronger nominal verifier capacity alone does not ensure useful selective delivery.

Matched critic continuation on 52 questions/15 report groups yields control 3/19/30 and crossed 3/15/34 by reported scoring; the wrong-rate interval includes zero. Against the stronger original critic's 4/22/26, crossed loses one correct delivery and fails the no-correct-loss rule. Repair changes no final outcomes relative to saved no-repair prefixes. A reserved 49-question/15-report diagnostic yields control 4/17/28 versus crossed 4/9/36 under reported scoring, but raw correct delivery falls from eight to seven. Selecting only the favorable metric would conceal failure of the both-metric adoption criterion. SAS and additional baseline conditions are all retained in the condition census below.

The matched SAS continuation on the same 52-question selection cohort changes reported outcomes from 9/20/23 (control) to 9/10/33 (crossed), but raw correct delivery decreases from 11 to ten. The unadapted analysis/demonstration baseline gives 9/24/19 reported and 10/23/19 raw. Thus the crossed SAS can improve wrong delivery against that baseline without an observed correct-count loss, yet still fails the both-metric no-loss comparison against the matched trained control. These are different reference systems, not interchangeable success claims.

On a controlled 12-report/24-program mutation probe, a trained critic accepts 11 of 12 reference programs and zero mutations; the plain reviewer withholds all. A question-blind depth-three program-feature tree scores 275/382 on original synthetic validation, and an extended-corpus tree 295/382 on its own validation; both exceed the 50\% majority baseline without seeing questions. On the reused natural24 pool, original/extended trees yield reported 5/8/11 and 5/5/14, versus the original neural critic's 11/4/9. These positive neural and shallow-control results show both discriminative potential and shortcut opportunities, not proof of neural shortcut use or broad semantic understanding. The neural gain remains development evidence.

A source-header materialization prototype adds 183 catalog quantities while preserving all 1,922 original quantities across 73 fitting packets, and restores reference operand coverage on four questions. A proposed 292-case-arm neural comparison was prepared but not run. Executable source support is verified; a QA gain is not measured. It is not counted as a completed method success.

External exploratory controls use original unadapted recipes: MortarBench local typed matching is 39/90 for SAS and 26/90 for MAS, based on partial JSON/XML evidence from nine prepared loan cases; this is not the official full benchmark protocol. The FRAMES closed-book control gives 2/100 and 1/100 under strict matching, unchanged by punctuation sensitivity; evidence URLs are not retrieved. These controls do not establish retrieval or multi-hop generalization. Their scoring and exposure limitations prevent treating them as confirmatory evidence for the adapted financial program systems.

\subsection{Windows Candidate and Verifier Pilots}
The initial planned 24-pair Windows candidate comparison stops after five complete pairs for post-hoc diagnosis. Refusal-allowed yields five refusals. Forced attempts yield zero reported-correct and one raw-correct candidate; a forced-one-step follow-up on the same five yields two reported-correct and one raw-correct. The original reviewer withholds everything. Removing contradictory repair fields or using a verdict-only 0.5B reviewer accepts all five (two reported-correct, three wrong); a 3B verdict-only reviewer withholds all. This exposes a local schema-policy problem but not semantic discrimination.

On four new reports with reference/near-miss candidates, the 0.5B reviewer accepts all eight and the 3B reviewer withholds all eight. Forced 3B comparison selects the reference in five of eight order presentations, only two of four reports in both orders. On six new reports with a reject-both option, a reversed-order agreement rule yields three correct, zero wrong and nine withheld across the two pair types per report. These dependent, reference-enriched challenges do not estimate natural-pool delivery risk.

The next six-report natural-pair pilot produces no correct final candidate in either draft and withholds all six. A separate six-report base/adapted pilot produces zero versus one correct candidate, yet agreement delivers zero correct, two wrong and withholds four. Thus the apparently error-free constructed result fails to transfer. A six-report adapted-3B versus unadapted-7B program pilot produces one versus zero correct final candidates, with more resources used by 7B; an answer-key prefix selector could recover four reports, but no deployable endpoint selector is demonstrated.

\subsection{Endpoint, Retrieval, Precision and Catalog Controls}
Model-predicted operation count on 16 reports changes official correct candidates from one to three (three gains, one loss). A gold-count control also yields three, on exactly the same recovered reports; gold count adds no recovery beyond predicted count on that slice. A retrospective endpoint selector makes no change in its initial test. On the 96-report matched precision cohort, a first-step rule changes Q4 official accuracy from 2 to 8 and Q8 from 2 to 6. On the earlier 24 reports it instead reduces Q4 from 3 to 1 and Q8 from 5 to 1. This cross-cohort reversal prevents a general recommendation to truncate at the first operation. The answer-key prefix oracle reaches at most 11/96 per arm, leaving most errors outside prefix selection.

Unused operations occur in 67/96 Q4 versus 14/96 Q8 outputs (and 20/24 versus 7/24 in the earlier cohort). Slicing preserves final execution results; it does not create an accuracy gain. Precision raises initial official matching from 3/24 to 5/24 (one loss, three gains; $p=0.625$), with unchanged 3/24 normalized symbolic matches. Its 96-report replication is 2 versus 2; reported scoring also ties but with one discordance in each direction rather than the two official discordances each way. Q8 raises peak commit from about 2.59 to 3.94 GB and median latency from 20.6 to 32.2 seconds in that replication.

Retrieval screens on 883 development questions cover all annotated spans for lexical8 750, BM25 748, reciprocal-rank fusion 733, BGE-small 728 and BGE/lexical hybrid 748. Wider lexical retrieval covers 827, adding 77 with no losses, at a larger token allowance (median evidence/catalog tokens 711 to 1,225). Numerical reference bindability rises 703 to 767; source identity and model success remain separate. The pilot's 1/16 to 4/16 generation gain ($p=0.25$) fails directionally at 2/21 to 1/21 ($p=1$). The latter company-cluster interval is $[-20.8,+11.1]$ percentage points, and median latency rises 18.9 to 26.3 seconds. A fixed-catalog follow-up was proposed to separate additional text from catalog options, but no completed result is identified in the retained retrieval evidence; it is not counted as an executed ablation.

Catalog completion makes 28 additional annotated test programs numerically bindable in its source screen. Five enriched neural cases improve 0 to 2; on the unselected shared 96 reports accuracy changes 2 to 1, despite reference-executable witnesses increasing 82 to 84. On the separate 15-report oracle matrix, repeated unrestricted controls reproduce their plans. All four structure-only successes remain correct with the shortlist. Structure plus shortlist yields ten official and seven reported successes; it fixes multiple variables simultaneously and is neither a deployable method nor a single-factor mechanism attribution.

\subsection{Baseline Capacity, Format, Review and Reference Checks}
The initial 16-context TAT-QA format pilot gives 1/16 for both 3B direct and program output, on different questions. The 40-context direct-capacity comparison gives 5/40 for 3B and 6/40 for 7B (four 3B-only, five 7B-only); a 120-context replication gives 12 versus 29 (five versus 22 unique successes). Gold-scale substitution rescues 11 wrong 3B and five wrong 7B answers in that replication, showing that scale selection matters but does not explain the full gap. This is an answer-key sensitivity, not deployed accuracy.

Fixed-7B format controls favor direct output on FinQA's shared reported metric, 35/96 versus 20/96, but favor programs with substantial uncertainty on TAT-QA, 19/72 versus 14/72. The FinQA agreement gate retains only three correct, two wrong and withholds 91, losing 17 of 20 reported-correct programs. Its percentage-label subgroup is direct 23/53 versus program 19/53; other supported numerical labels are 12/40 versus 1/40; three unsupported labels receive no match. The original official program score 1/96, converted sensitivity 19/96 and reported score 20/96 are retained separately.

The initial trained-critic replay on 16 wider-evidence candidates appears favorable under reported scoring: control keeps three correct with zero wrong. The transfer replay on 21 narrow candidates has an executable-only baseline of two correct/19 wrong; control yields 1/5/15, crossed 1/2/18. Both lose one correct candidate, and two of three crossed-accepted outputs are wrong. Independent 7B recomputation on the initial 16 yields four correct answers alone, but the agreement gate withholds all; one overlapping correct answer pair differs beyond the frozen tolerance despite matching the reported label separately.

On 72 separate TAT-QA contexts, actor adaptation improves candidates from 3 to 8, and review delivers 6/10/56; ten of 16 accepted answers are wrong. A resident 21-report workload produces six official-correct (five reported-correct) candidates and delivers 5/4/12 under both scores. Disabling RAM prompt caching preserves all plans/verdicts/deliveries, reduces sampled RSS from 5.852 to 5.013 GB and job peak from 6.101 to 5.264 GB. The default-cache job logs failed allocation and fallback, whereas the disabled-cache run logs none. A failed-allocation peak does not identify exactly how many bytes were successfully committed.

A temporal growth rule applied to 453 questions from 117 report-disjoint test reports delivers only three, none matching either benchmark score. Individual discrepancies include plausible financial interpretations and incompatible reference conventions; they are not adjudicated gold-label corrections. A broader consistency screen reproduces every stored annotated execution result for 883 development and 1,147 test questions, then flags reported/raw incompatibility on 110/851 eligible development labels and 129/1,100 eligible test labels. These are screening flags, not 239 proven annotation errors. Official primary labels and all cases are retained.

\subsection{Planner Development and Held-Out Result}
The initial 12-report prototype produces a usable shape on only one report; 11 predictions are constant-only. Revising the grammar to unbound VALUE slots yields usable shapes on 11/12. Official final correct deliveries remain three in both arms; wrong decreases four to three and withheld increases five to six. Reported-correct delivery falls five to four. Median latency rises 23.0 to 30.9 seconds. This is development evidence for producing valid shapes, not an accuracy gain. The revision precedes held-out inference.

In the main 96-context cohort the four correct-delivery gains have no losses, but wrong delivery rises two and candidate correctness falls one. The main paper reports this failed practical criterion prominently. At 28 secondary contexts there is no correct-delivery change, and two correct candidates are lost. The incomplete 7B test prefix and recovery attempt remain archived; completion of MAS does not imply completion of the four-arm comparison.
